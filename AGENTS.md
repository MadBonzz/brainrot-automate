# Making an episode in a Codex terminal session

Take the user's premise through to a playable vertical MP4. Use the requested length, or about 45 seconds by default. The standard episode contract lives in `film/ensemble.py`. Complete plans include `example/last-clap/`, `example/floor-ten/` and `example/bell-bay/`. The timecoded observations in `docs/shorts-reference-study.md` explain the choices behind the newest pilot.

1. Start with competing needs, the object each person interprets differently and the opening joke that will come back. Write a first-person story around an observable rule. Show how people have adapted their jobs, family lives or customs to it. Give at least four residents distinct interests. Let the audience see the ordinary approach fail, discover a counterexample and understand what the protagonist stands to lose. End with a visible consequence and a joke or image that pays off an earlier detail.
2. Make a unique folder under `plans/`. Write `story.json` and `direction.json` using the ensemble schemas. Invent landmarks and practical props for this particular town. Keep positions and ownership consistent. Give actors independent tasks, eyelines and gesture windows. Use a life setting for background residents and choose accent, cycle or sustain for the action. Use short bursts of discovery and longer holds for choices. Put the evidence in its own shot when a wide view would hide it. Use spoken phrase anchors for reveals. List reusable performances with `python movie.py --list-actions`. Review a new authored pose with `tools/review_motions.py`.
3. Render the boards with `python movie.py --from-plan <folder> --quality review --storyboard`. Inspect the numbered pages inside the printed storyboard folder. Check whether a viewer can follow the public example, failed attempt, exception, cost and ending from the images. Correct unclear blocking, framing or impossible prop contacts in the job's direction.
4. Run `python movie.py --resume <job-folder> --quality final` or use the user's requested review quality. The runner uses the local narrator, Blender and FFmpeg and measures the rendered frame sequence. Report the playable file and measured duration.

An outer Codex session can write the plans directly. The standalone Python entry point calls the configured Codex CLI for a draft, an editorial review, the world and short blocks of direction. For a JSON-only request with an output schema, respond with that JSON.

5. Screen the **encoded movie** with its sound on. Every ensemble render supplies `screening.html` beside the MP4. Play the film from the opening through the ending once, then replay the timestamped lines. Record what is visible in the frame. Check the owner's hands before and after a handoff, the side of a closed door, support and weight in a seated pose, and the cause of the next action. Listen for repeated events, stray words and conclusions introduced before their evidence. Use “Needs revision” for unclear actions. After fixing a shot, reopen the new encode and review that line and the cuts on either side. Compare difficult movements with the relevant reference interval.
6. For picture that needs individual blocking, supply `screenplay.json` next to a saved ensemble plan, or pass `--screenplay path/to/screenplay.json` with `--resume`. Give each line an `id`, `say`, observable `evidence`, and one or more `shots`. Each camera selection can choose a one-based `beat` and optional fractional `range` or a relative `take` JSON describing native Blender actors, geometry, tracks, cameras and prop holds. The recording sets its cut length. The corrected MP4, cue player and decoded-speech verification are in the quality folder under `screened`. The screening notes identify the exact encode by its SHA-256 digest.
7. When reviewing a generated episode through the signed-in CLI, use `python -m film.visual_review <movie.mp4>`. Read its per-line findings before delivery. Replay unclear physical contacts and contradictions at their actual timestamps in the encoded movie, fix the staging or wording, and screen the updated movie.
